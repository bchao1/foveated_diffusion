In Figure \ref{fig:baseline_distilled}, we provide extended baseline comparisons on the
step- and guidance-distilled
\href{https://huggingface.co/black-forest-labs/FLUX.2-klein-4B}{FLUX.2-klein-4B} model,
where all five arms run at $4$ denoising steps with $\text{CFG}=1.0$. The high-resolution region defined by the foveation mask is a circle
with radius $0.33$ relative to the image diagonal and a randomly placed center.

Our method consistently outperforms the na\"ive mixed-resolution baseline and the two
token-reduction baselines, all of which retain the boundary seams, scale inconsistencies,
and structural distortions they exhibit on the undistilled model---these artifacts stem
from the spatial resampling itself rather than from the length of the denoising
trajectory, so shortening the trajectory does not remove them. Most importantly, our
method achieves perceptually indistinguishable quality from the full high-resolution
distilled baseline while using approximately $62\%$ fewer tokens, resulting in a
$2.05\times$ speedup in denoising time and a $1.73\times$ speedup end to end; the gap
between the two is the VAE decode, which we do not foveate and which occupies a larger
share of the runtime once only $4$ steps remain. Notably, our foveation LoRA is trained on
the undistilled model and is plugged into the distilled model untouched, with no
retraining and no degradation relative to the undistilled setting. Step distillation
shortens the denoising trajectory while foveation reduces the tokens processed at each
step---orthogonal axes whose speedups compose multiplicatively, so Foveated Diffusion
delivers a further $2.05\times$ on top of an already heavily accelerated $4$-step,
guidance-free model at no additional training cost.
